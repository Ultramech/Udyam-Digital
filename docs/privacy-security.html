Udyam Digital — privacy & security note

A privacy-first prototype built around consent, purpose limitation and data minimisation, in the spirit of India's DPDP Act, 2023. It does not claim legal compliance or certification — that needs a formal review. All business records here are synthetic (the case's dataset); the live demo used the team's own phone and Aadhaar with consent.

Consent and the owner's rights

  • Consent before collection, enforced in code. KYC APIs return 403 without a recorded KYC consent; DigiLocker has its own optional purpose; WhatsApp/Telegram updates need the "service" purpose. Each receipt stores id, notice version, purposes and time.
  • Purpose-bound codes. A login OTP can never sign an order; a signing code is bound to the SHA-256 of the exact PDF shown.
  • Minimal sharing. Share links show plan and price only — no phone, GSTIN or address. A CA uploads through a delegate link without an account and sees nothing else.
  • Access, export, erase. Account → "Your data & privacy": download everything as JSON, or delete the account in one tap (profile, businesses, chats, plans, KYC, consent, support). Orders are contract records and are kept anonymised.

AI boundaries

  • Gemini (paid API, server-side) gets the owner's words and, after KYC consent, the document pages — never the phone number. Prompts aren't used for model training under Google's paid-tier terms.
  • AI output is untrusted input: facts are schema- and range-checked, intents whitelisted, and any number the rules engine didn't produce gets the reply discarded. Price, eligibility and KYC verdicts are deterministic code.

Security controls

  • Fail closed. No key → the service refuses (503) instead of pretending: OTP, payments, document reading, channels, DigiLocker. Test shortcuts exist only behind ALLOW_TEST_VERIFY=1, never in production.
  • Verifiable money and signatures. Razorpay signatures HMAC-checked and the payment re-read (order, exact amount, status). Signed order forms carry the PDF hash, time, masked phone and IP.
  • Authenticated webhooks. Meta X-Hub-Signature-256, Twilio signature, Telegram secret token — constant-time compare, retries de-duplicated.
  • Access control. Ops roles enforced server-side and audit-logged; forged staff cookies are refused; tracking links are unguessable tokens; sales credit only from a signed-in staff session.
  • Abuse limits. OTP: 30 s cooldown, 4 sends/hour, 5 attempts, 5-minute expiry, single use. Chat: 12 messages/minute.
  • Masking. GSTIN, Udyam, phone and ID numbers masked in UI and logs; logs never hold message text, phone numbers or tokens.
  • Hosting. Vercel functions pinned to Mumbai (bom1); keys live only in server environment variables; database is Turso (libSQL).
Known gaps (honest list) before production: field-level encryption at rest for KYC fields and phone numbers (KMS); an automated retention/purge job (today's daily cron only sends reminders and flags stuck orders); a formal DPIA and DPDP legal review; IP-level rate limiting at the edge; Aadhaar eSign through a licensed ASP (today: IT-Act e-signature, OTP-bound and hashed); live GST/Udyam APIs instead of the supplied registries.
